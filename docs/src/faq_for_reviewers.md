# Questions a reviewer is likely to ask

**How accurate is the range, and at what distances?** On the Mid-Air test flights (synthetic, so the truth is exact), the terrain-from-motion method has a median distance error of {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_median|.1%}} (95 % interval {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_median_ci.0|.1%}} to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_median_ci.1|.1%}}), with {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_valid|.0%}} of estimates valid, but one estimate in ten is more than {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_p90|.0%}} off. A flat ground plane with a take-off altimeter, the simple approach, has a median error of {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_alt_median|.0%}}. On real footage (AU-AIR) there is no ground truth, so only repeatability is measured: stationary vehicles' positions repeat to {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_rel_all|.1%}} of their distance. Ranges tested are short (low-altitude flight); no long-range claim is made. Evidence: `results/midair/test/terrain/`, `results/auair/repeatability_view_held_out_B2_yolo26n_960.json`.

**What happens near the horizon?** Errors grow quickly as the line of sight flattens. Below 2° of depression, error propagation becomes non-linear (the Monte Carlo prediction is {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.mc_over_fo_below_2deg|.1f}} times the first-order one), and on AU-AIR the repeatability worsens from {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_rel_dep_20_40|.1%}} of range at 20-40° to {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_rel_dep_0_10|.1%}} below 10°. Rays at or above the horizon are reported as invalid, never ranged. Evidence: `results/midair/test/propagation.json`, D-517.

**Does it work on slopes?** A flat plane does not: its error grows from {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_err_slope_0_5|.1%}} on slopes under 5° to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_err_slope_45_90|.1%}} above 45°. Terrain from motion builds a local ground surface from tracked image features and the drone's known motion. Its error stays between {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_err_slope_first|.1%}} and {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_err_slope_30_45|.1%}} on slopes up to 45°, and rises to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_err_slope_last|.1%}} on slopes over 45° (still well below the flat plane there). Evidence: `results/midair/test/slope.json`, `results/midair/test/terrain/binned.json`.

**Is the uncertainty honest, and how do you know?** Not fully. The error bars were checked against the truth on flights not used to fit them: after calibration (split conformal prediction, fitted on separate calibration flights), nominal 68 %, 90 % and 95 % intervals contain the truth {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_68|.0%}}, {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_90|.0%}} and {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_95|.0%}} of the time, against {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_raw_90|.0%}} for 90 % before calibration. So they are close but still a little over-confident on the test flights. Evidence: `results/midair/test/terrain/calibration.json`, D-403.

**What does the reliability flag catch and miss?** It is a small logistic model on interpretable features (local ground roughness relative to range is the strongest). On test flights its area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) is {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_auroc|.2f}}: it flags {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_recall|.0%}} of the estimates that are more than 10 % wrong, and {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_precision|.0%}} of what it flags is wrong by that much. The large errors it does not flag are the rest, so it is a useful warning, not a guarantee. Evidence: `results/midair/test/terrain/flag.json`, D-404.

**What assumptions does the camera model make on real footage?** AU-AIR publishes no camera calibration. The focal length ({{RESULT:headline.json#auair.focal_px|.0f}} pixels) was measured from the drone's GPS motion against image motion on calibration flights and checked against car sizes; the principal point is assumed at the image centre; lens distortion is handled by using the central image only where it matters; the camera tilt is not recorded, so it is estimated from image motion (and checked against the visible horizon); the altimeter's zero drifts and was corrected where it could be measured. All of this is in `docs/auair_camera_model.md`.

**What latency, on what hardware, at what percentile?** On a laptop {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.cpu|s}} CPU, the most accurate setting runs at {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.accurate_fps|.1f}} frames per second ({{RESULT:headline.json#edge.accurate_median_ms|.0f}} ms per frame, median). The fast setting sustains {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_fps|.1f}} frames per second by running detection on every second frame and overlapping detection with tracking; each frame's result then arrives {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_latency_median_ms|.0f}} ms after it is read (median; 99th percentile {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_latency_p99_ms|.0f}} ms). Evidence: `results/edge/pareto.json`, `results/edge/figures/pareto.png`.

**How was test data kept separate from tuning?** Every dataset has a split decided before results were seen (`results/splits.json`, D-506). Test splits ran once with `--final` after the settings were frozen; `scripts/freeze.py` recorded the hash of every setting file and the test queues stopped if any changed. Code fixes made during a test run (a crash on an empty result) changed no setting and are recorded (D-607). Evidence: `results/frozen/`, `docs/decisions.md`.

**Why not use a laser rangefinder or radar, and how would this combine with one?** This project asks how far a passive camera alone can go, and how honestly it can report its own uncertainty. An active sensor measures range far better; the natural combination is to use the camera for detection, tracking and bearing, and the rangefinder for range, with this pipeline's per-object uncertainty used to weight the two in the same Kalman filter, or to take over when the active sensor drops out.

**What would you do differently with real sensor telemetry?** Record the camera tilt and lens calibration (they had to be inferred here), use a properly referenced altitude (the altimeter drifted by metres within a flight), time-stamp camera and flight data together, and use a terrain model where one exists. Each would remove one of the largest error sources measured here.

**What is the weakest part of this work?** On real footage there is no ground truth for range or speed, so the real-footage results are repeatability and plausibility only, and {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.vehicle_share_above_70|.0%}} of vehicle speeds are implausibly high. On the tracking side, small people are rarely tracked ({{RESULT:headline.json#visdrone_test.person_recall_16_32|.0%}} at 16 to 32 pixels). And 30 frames per second is reached only in throughput, at a cost in accuracy, not in per-frame delay.
